Foothold

Build a forward operating base from bare desert.

Start with a small convoy and a patch of flattened ground. Prepare the ground, fly in the materials, raise the walls, and grow a patrol base into an air base with its own runway, helicopters and missions. Every structure is built by your engineers, and every load comes in by road or air.

The game

You are the base commander and its engineer.

Foothold is about building, logistics and keeping a garrison going, not about steering soldiers in a firefight. The setting is a fictional desert country, with the kit of the US Army around 2009-2011.

01

Construction is physical

Every structure needs prepared ground, the right machines, materials on site and time. Nothing appears out of thin air.

02

Logistics is the bottleneck

You start with what a convoy can carry. After that, everything comes by helicopter, by road or, one day, by your own runway.

03

Macro, not micro

Plan the base, order the work, send the missions. Your units find their way, refuel, rearm and go home on their own.

Build

From bare ground to a walled base

The ground is a grid of 2 m tiles. Dozers level it chunk by chunk, then your engineers raise each structure in stages: HESCO walls, guard towers, tents and B-huts, generators, fuel points, motor pools and hangars.

  • Over 70 structures, each with its own materials and build stages
  • Dozers clear, front loaders move the gravel, telehandlers stack the HESCO
  • Tear something down and you get half its materials back
Supply

Everything comes by road or air

Missions earn points; points buy materials and new units. Black Hawks and Chinooks bring loads in to your landing zones, trucks drive the track in from the edge of the map, and at an air base C-17s land with whole pallets.

  • A landing zone, a parking bay or a runway decides what can reach you
  • Fuel, ammunition, spare parts and medical supplies keep the garrison going
Garrison

A base that lives

Squads need homes, from tents to B-huts to containerised housing. Vehicles have their own bays. After a mission everyone comes back needing something: fuel, ammunition, repairs, the aid station. Without power, the facilities stop and the lights go out.

  • MRAPs, Strykers, Bradleys, artillery, helicopters and aeroplanes
  • Each vehicle modelled from photos of the real one
The valley

The valley pushes back

The base sits in a valley full of villages and dirt roads. At night the enemy plants IEDs on the roads you use. Route clearance opens new roads and finds what's hidden on them; patrols and meetings with village leaders keep the villages on your side.

  • Roads open one section at a time, from the main supply route out
  • Enemy activity can be turned off for a pure builder
Missions

Missions that run themselves

Every base level adds a cycle of missions the base sends on its own: patrols, convoys, fire missions, air support. Keep the units ready and the streaks unbroken to reach the next level. Special missions open new ground: a land agreement, an old minefield to clear, the airfield extension.

Air base

Your own airfield

Lay a runway, taxiways, hangars and hardstands. Aeroplanes taxi out, wait for the runway, take off and come back to their own stands. The Shadow drone is shot off its launcher; C-17s, tankers and bombers need the 1,024 m strip.

Day and night

Lit windows and floodlights

A full day cycle: hard desert light at noon, long shadows at dusk, and at night a base lit only where it has power. Engines, rotors, wind, birds by day and crickets at night.

Progression

Five base levels

Each level unlocks new structures, units and missions, and asks for a bigger, better-run base.

Level 0

Patrol base

A convoy, a dozer and a patch of ground. Walls, towers, tents, a generator, a landing zone and the gate.

Level 1

COP

Walled and guarded. B-huts, Black Hawks and Kiowas, route clearance teams, and the first IEDs on the roads.

Level 2

FOB

A battalion's garrison: Strykers and Bradleys, Chinooks and Apaches, artillery, hangars and drones, and a land agreement that opens ground for an airfield.

Level 3

Main operating base

A brigade hub: containerised housing, the old minefield cleared, an 800 m runway, a control tower, transports and jets.

Level 4

Air base

The goal: a 1,024 m runway for C-17s, tankers and bombers, and every mission cycle running.

Updates

Latest builds

The newest changes, as they appear in the game's main menu.

0.1.34

8 Nov 2026
  • Quitting to the main menu no longer leaves its background running at the game's speed.
  • Aeroplanes sound like aeroplanes: jets roar and propeller aircraft buzz. They used to make helicopter noises.
  • Mortar rounds aimed at a village near the base no longer land inside the base: they fall on the far side of the village.
  • Only refusals and warnings show in red in the log: "moving to the new helipad" and the like are plain news.
  • Hangars, hardstands and helipads list the aircraft that can live on them, always up to date. The Shadow lives on its launcher, not in a small hangar.
  • With more than one ECP, missions leave through the one nearest the road in.
  • An armory, aid station or other facility with no power shows the no-power sign, even before the base has a generator.
  • A squad that left on a mission from inside its home no longer shows up frozen at the door if that home is removed while it's away.
  • Requests count in ones: every click adds 1 of what the row shows (1 gal, 1 bag, 1 t of gravel), Shift 10, Ctrl 100, both 1,000. Prices are for one, so cheap things cost a fraction of a point; what an order costs hasn't changed.
  • Water and MREs are hidden until the base has a use for them.
  • Roads and paths can be laid through the ECP's lane, under it or after it's built.

0.1.33

8 Nov 2026
  • Removing an armory, fuel point, repair point or aid station while a unit was on its way no longer stops resupply for the whole base: the unit goes to another, or to a new one once it's built.

0.1.32

7 Nov 2026
  • The aid station can be built at a patrol base. Mortars and the gate surge come back with wounded, and with nowhere to treat them those squads could never go again, so a COP was out of reach.
  • A vehicle on a mission can no longer hold up its cycle for ever: one that gets stuck or drives in circles is put at the end of its route.
  • Mortar sections walk out to their mortar pit for a fire mission, and the rounds leave from the pit: they used to come out of the tent. Afterwards guns go home instead of staying where they were.
  • A road ordered under a parked vehicle no longer makes the vehicle vanish: it backs out and comes back afterwards. Loading a save gives back any vehicle lost this way, in its own bay.
  • Vehicles no longer get stuck going round in circles at a gap or at their parking bay. If one ever does get nowhere for a while, it's moved along its route.
  • A vehicle saved on its way to its bay no longer stays where it was after loading: it carries on to the bay.
  • On a mortar fire mission every mortar pit on the base fires, not just the one the mortar section walks to.
